## 5 — Discernment

**Lead (must lead the user-facing reading):**
"You are standing at the boundary between knowing something and deciding what responsibility
that knowledge creates."

**Supporting language only (never the headline):** separate what you know, what you feel, and
what you assume.

## The 5 × 1 situation

Someone knows something they believe a friend deserves to know, but telling the truth could cost
the friendship. The reading must be able to reach these recognitions without prescribing a decision:

- Knowing something is not the same as knowing what responsibility that knowledge creates.
- Why do you believe this person needs to hear it from you?
- Would you still believe it needed to be told if telling it cost you the relationship?

**1 Beginning complements it as:** something has become impossible to leave unexamined; the
beginning is not necessarily acting, but honestly naming the first question before deciding what
the answer requires.

## Standard for the remaining numbers

Depth first, then distinction. Reveal the psychological/relational structure and offer a
recognition or question. Never a canned instruction. Never expose the semantic vocabulary or the
derivation rules as the explanation.
